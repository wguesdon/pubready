The calibration of the predicted probabilities was assessed against the observed outcome event_1y = yes (events, n = 130) versus no (non-events, n = 370). For each model, the Brier score is the mean squared difference between the prediction and the outcome. The calibration intercept (calibration-in-the-large) is the intercept of a logistic regression of the outcome with logit(p) as an offset, and the calibration slope is the coefficient of logit(p) in a logistic regression, each with a 95% Wald CI; ideal values are 0 and 1. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test grouped the subjects at the deciles of the prediction (g = 10; tied deciles merge, df = groups - 2). The figure shows the observed proportion against the mean prediction in each group with a 95% Wilson CI, a lowess smooth of the outcome on the prediction (f = 2/3, without robustness iterations, which would treat the events of a binary outcome as outliers), and the spike histogram of the predictions. risk_clinical: Brier score 0.172, calibration intercept -0.14 (95% CI -0.36 to 0.07), slope 0.82 (95% CI 0.59 to 1.05), Hosmer-Lemeshow chi-square(8) = 9.02, P = 0.34, observed/expected 0.92; risk_extended: Brier score 0.169, calibration intercept -0.01 (95% CI -0.25 to 0.23), slope 0.62 (95% CI 0.47 to 0.78), Hosmer-Lemeshow chi-square(8) = 26.96, P < 0.001, observed/expected 0.99.
